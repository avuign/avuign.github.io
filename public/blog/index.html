What is my PhD about?

My PhD explores two questions: what exactly are quantum field theories, and what is spacetime made of?

What is spacetime made of?#

I’m trying to understand what are the building blocks of space and time themselves. As strange as this question might sound, the last 50 years of fundamental physics taught us that instead of being fundamental, spacetime can be an emergent phenomenon, much like temperature emerges from the motion of a large number of particles. What are the “particles” building up spacetime? How exactly do they interact?

Those are the questions I’m exploring in my research, where I essentially study the simplest models I can think of that exhibit gravity-like properties when we look at a large ensemble of particles. This is the core idea behind the holographic principle.

Finding such models is quite hard, but we know some examples coming out of string theory. I study what I think are the simplest of those models in terms of complexity, and try to understand why exactly those models are holographic. Understanding what is the crucial mechanism of particle interactions that leads to an emergent geometric description satisfying the laws of general relativity would be a fundamental breakthrough in the field.

What are quantum field theories?#

Quantum Field Theory (QFT) is a wonderful machine that we think describes everything about the universe. Why is the sky blue? What are magnets? What are the building blocks of matter? Why does the universe expand? All those questions and many more are answered by performing a QFT calculation. When we can do this calculation we are able to compare the theory with experiment and the result is striking. Both results agree up to 14 digits, making QFT one of the most accurate scientific inventions of humanity.

However, most of the time, we don’t know how to do this calculation. This led us to turn the question around, and instead of asking what does the theory predict, we ask what can it not predict. We ask what is the most general answer that is compatible with very general physics principles, such as causality or the fact that probabilities sum up to 1. Surprisingly enough, such broad principles are enough to constrain the space of QFTs, an idea known as the S-Matrix bootstrap program.

Studying what comes out after imposing all those constraints might ultimately teach us about the things we still don’t know about the most fundamental building blocks of the universe, and in particular teach us about Color confinement, which would lead to an incredible technological step forward.
