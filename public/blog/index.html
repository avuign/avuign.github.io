What is my PhD about?

My PhD explores two questions: what exactly are quantum field theories, and what is spacetime made of?

What is spacetime made of?#

The force of gravity in our universe is a consequence of the curvature of space and time themselves. This is what Albert Einstein told us already more than 100 years ago. Instead of being the scene on which everything happen, space and time combine into complex geometrical structures, and the motion on that geomety is what we perceive as the force of gravity.

I’m trying to understand what are the building blocks of space and time themselves. As strange as this question might sound, the last 50 years of fundamental physics taught us that instead of being fundamental, spacetime can be an emergent phenomenon, much like temperature emerges from the motion of a large number of particles. What are the “particles” building up spacetime? How exactly do they interact?

Those are the questions I’m exploring in my research, where I essentially study the simplest models I can think of that exhibit an emergent spacetime when we look at a large ensemble of particles. Maybe suprisingly, the dimension of that spacetime is higher than the dimension in which those particles live. This is the core idea behind the holographic principle.

Finding such models is quite hard, but we know some examples coming out of string theory. I study what I think are the simplest of those models in terms of complexity, and try to understand why exactly those models are holographic. Understanding what is the crucial mechanism of particle interactions that leads to an emergent geometric description satisfying the laws of general relativity would be a fundamental breakthrough in the field.

The main open problem is that we don’t know how to think of our interaction of gravity in the language of quantum mechanics, which is the language in which we understand all the other interactions in our universe. For the imaginary worlds that I study, we understand quantum gravity because it emerges from usual quantum particles that we understand. I believe that by understand better why gravity emerges in those cases, we will learn how to build concrete models for quantum gravity in our world. Such a theory will teach us about some of the most mysterious objects in the universe, the black holes, but also about the beginning of time itself, the Big Bang.

What are quantum field theories?#

Quantum Field Theory (QFT) is a wonderful machine that we think describes everything about the universe. What are the building blocks of matter? What are magnets? Why does the universe expand? Why is the sky blue? All those questions and many more are answered by performing a QFT calculation. When we can do this calculation and that we are able to compare the theory with experiment, the result is striking. The theory agrees with experiment up to 14 digits, making QFT one the most accurate scientific discovery of humanity.

QFT works beautifully when we can do this calculation. Its best incarnation is the Standard Model of Particle Physics that describes all the interactions in our world with unprecedented accuracy. All those interactions can be reduced to 3 fundamental interactions : the electromagnetic interaction, responsible for electricity and magnetism, the weak interaction, responsible for atomic decays and radioactivity, and the strong interactions, responsible for holding the innermost parts of atoms glued to each other, also called Color confinement. We know how to do the calculations for electromagnetism and the weak force, but not for the strong force. Everything we tried to solve those equations failed miserably, but we know it’s the correct equation because we can approximate the solution with a computer and it reproduces the stable atoms we know.

Instead of trying to directly solve the equation, we then try to turn the question around, and instead of asking what does the theory predict, we ask what can it not predict. We ask what is the most general answer that is compatible with very general physics principles, such as causality (the cause precedes the effect) or the fact that probabilities sum up to 1. Surprisingly enough, such broad principles are enough to non trivially constrain the space of QFTs, an idea known as the S-Matrix bootstrap program. This approach is strong enough to teach us lessons about the general structure of QFTs, and more pragmatically it can also predicts the existence of new particles.

Understanding more precisely the strong force would teach us a lot about materials, and in particular superconductors. With this knowledge we could store electricity indefinitely and completely solve many of the issues related to the storage of renewable energies. It would also probably teach us some deeper things about the true nature of QFT, which is still elusive to this day.
